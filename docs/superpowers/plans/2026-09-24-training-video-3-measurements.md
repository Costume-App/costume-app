```markdown
# Measurements, VO script

Voice: Ava (en-US-AvaMultilingualNeural). Recorded against the Demo Theatre Co. org.

## Measurements (`intro`)

In the last video, we cast Twelfth Night. Now it is time to measure everyone. We will fill in a performer's measurements, see where each one is taken, move quickly from person to person, and bring in forms that were filled in on paper.

## Open a performer (`open-performer`)

Measurements live with each performer. Open the show, choose a role, and go to its "Cast & Measure" tab.

> click: Twelfth Night, the Viola row, "Cast & Measure"

Choose the performer's name to open their measurement page.

> click: "Maya Brooks"; hold on her page, point at "0 of 20 measured"

## Enter measurements (`enter-measurements`)

Type each measurement into its box. There is no save button: a value saves as soon as you move to the next box, and a small dot beside it confirms it.

> type Height 5 ft 6 in, Chest / bust 34, then Tab; zoom the "Saved" dot

Sizes like shirt, pant, and shoe are free text, so write them the way the label on the clothing does.

> type Shirt size "M"; point at the counter, now "4 of 20 measured" (read the real number off the page)

## Where to measure (`where-to-measure`)

Not sure where a measurement is taken? Open "Where do I measure?" to see a front and back body diagram.

> click: "Where do I measure?"

Click into any box, and its spot on the diagram lights up in red.

> focus "Nape to floor"; zoom the highlighted back-view marker; then focus "Inseam"

## Write it like you would on paper (`handwriting`)

You can enter a number the way you would write it by hand. Thirty four and a half, written with a fraction, saves as thirty four point five.

> type Waist "26 1/2", Tab; point at the saved value

On an iPad with an Apple Pencil, you can write straight into any box, and the iPad turns your handwriting into text. If the app cannot read a value, a red dot tells you to write it again.

> point at a field; optionally type an unreadable value and zoom the red dot, then fix it

## Move between performers (`switcher`)

When you are measuring a whole cast, the bars at the top and bottom of the page take you to the previous or next person.

> click: "Next ›"; hold on the next performer's page

Choose the name in the middle to jump straight to anyone, and to see how many measurements each person has so far. You can also skip anyone who is already fully measured.

> click the centre button; point at the list with counts and the "✓"; tick "Skip anyone fully measured"

## Import paper forms (`import-forms`)

Already have forms filled in on paper? Back on the production, choose "Import measurement forms," and pick a photo of each form.

> click: "Import measurement forms", "Choose photos" (file chooser handled by the recorder)

The app reads every photo and matches it to a performer by name. Before anything is saved, you check each value next to what is already saved, and untick anything you do not want.

> hold through "Reading 1 of 1…"; point at the matched performer, the "new" badges, the "26.5" row

When it looks right, choose "Import," and the measurements are saved to that performer.

> click: "Import 1 form"; zoom the "Imported … measurements." note

## Wrap up (`wrap-up`)

That is how you measure a cast: type or write values that save as you go, check where each one is taken, move through the cast one person at a time, and import the forms you already have on paper. In the next video, we will start designing costumes and planning what to make. Thanks for watching.
```
