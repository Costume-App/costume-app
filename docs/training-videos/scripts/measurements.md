# Measurements, VO script

Voice: Ava (en-US-AvaMultilingualNeural). Recorded against the Demo Theatre Co. org.

## Measurements (`intro`)

In the last video, we cast Twelfth Night, giving every role its performers. Now it is time to measure everyone, so the costumes actually fit. This video walks through the full measuring workflow, from your first number to a whole cast recorded. We will fill in a performer's measurements, see where each one is taken on the body, move quickly between every person in the show, and bring in forms that were already filled in on paper. By the end, every performer will be ready for costume creations.

## Open a performer (`open-performer`)

Measurements live with each performer, right alongside their casting. Open the show, choose a role, and go to its "Cast & Measure" tab, the same tab you used to cast the show.

> click: Twelfth Night, the Viola row, "Cast & Measure"

Choose the performer's name to open their measurement page. Each performer gets one page for every measurement they need, no matter how many roles they play.

> click: "Maya Brooks"; hold on her page, point at "0 of 20 measured"

## Enter measurements (`enter-measurements`)

Type each measurement into its box. There is no save button here: a value saves as soon as you move to the next box, and a small dot beside it confirms it saved. Numeric fields like chest and waist take a plain number of inches.

> type Height 5 ft 6 in, Chest / bust 34, then Tab; zoom the "Saved" dot

Sizes like shirt, pant, and shoe are free text instead, so write them the way the label on the clothing does. There is no strict format to match, so any wording you would use on a tag works fine.

> type Shirt size "M"; point at the counter (read the real number off the page)

## Where to measure (`where-to-measure`)

Not sure where a measurement is taken? Open "Where do I measure?" to see a front and back body diagram, shown side by side. Every measurement on the form has a matching spot on that diagram.

> click: "Where do I measure?"

Click into any box, and its spot on the diagram lights up in red, so you always know exactly where to put the tape measure. This works for every field, from the neck down to the inseam.

> focus "Nape to floor"; zoom the highlighted back-view marker; then focus "Inseam"

## Write it like you would on paper (`handwriting`)

You can enter a number the way you would write it by hand. Waist, twenty six and a half, written with a fraction, saves as twenty six point five, ready to use right away.

> type Waist "26 1/2", Tab; point at the saved value, "26.5"

On an iPad with an Apple Pencil, you can write straight into any box, and the iPad turns your handwriting into text. If the app cannot read a value, a red dot appears, and you simply write it again.

> type "abc" into Waist, Tab; zoom the red "Couldn't read that number" dot; then correct it to "26 1/2"

## Move between performers (`switcher`)

When you are measuring a whole cast, the bars at the top and bottom of the page take you to the previous or next person. You never need to go back to the cast list.

> click: "Next ›"; hold on the next performer's page

Choose the name in the middle to jump straight to anyone, and to see how many measurements each person has so far. You can sort that list "By role," "A to Z," or by "Order added." Turn on "Skip anyone fully measured," and Next only stops on people who still need it.

> click the centre button; point at the list with counts and the "✓" beside Jordan Lee; tick "Skip anyone fully measured"

## Import paper forms (`import-forms`)

Already have forms filled in on paper? Back on the production, choose "Import measurement forms," and pick a photo of each one, as a JPG, PNG, or PDF.

> click: "Import measurement forms", "Choose photos" (file chooser handled by the recorder)

The app reads every photo with AI and matches it to a performer by name. Before anything is saved, you check each value next to what is already saved, marked new, changed, or the same, and untick anything you do not want.

> hold through "Reading 1 of 1…"; point at the matched performer Rosa Diaz, the "new" badges, the "26.5" row

When it looks right, choose "Import," and the measurements are saved straight to that performer, with nothing to retype.

> click: "Import 1 form"; zoom the "Imported 12 measurements." note

## Wrap up (`wrap-up`)

That is how you measure a cast: type or write values that save as you go, check where each one is taken, move through the cast one person at a time, and import the forms you already have on paper. Along the way we opened a performer's page, entered numbers and sizes, checked the body diagram, wrote a value the way you would by hand, moved between performers with the switcher, and imported a paper form for Rosa Diaz. In the next video, we will move into Costume Creations, designing and tracking every piece for the show. Thanks for watching, and see you next time.
